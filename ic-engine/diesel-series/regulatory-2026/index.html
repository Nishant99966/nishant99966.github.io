European Union — Euro 7 heavy-duty

Regulation (EU) 2024/1257 is in force.

For categories M2, M3, N2 and N3 it applies:

  • from 29 May 2028 for new vehicle types;
  • from 29 May 2029 for new vehicles.

The heavy-duty exhaust table in Annex I includes:

Pollutant WHSC/WHTC RDE
NOx 200 mg/kWh 260 mg/kWh
PM 8 mg/kWh —
PN10 6×10^11 #/kWh 9×10^11 #/kWh
CO 1500 mg/kWh 1950 mg/kWh
NMOG 80 mg/kWh 105 mg/kWh
NH3 60 mg/kWh 85 mg/kWh
CH4 500 mg/kWh 650 mg/kWh
N2O 200 mg/kWh 260 mg/kWh

For heavy-duty test conditions the Regulation refers to WHTC/WHSC engine-bench testing through UN Regulation No. 49 and to RDE requirements.

For the heaviest N3 vehicles above 16 t and M3 vehicles above 7.5 t, the Regulation lists a main lifetime of 700,000 km or 12 years, with the additional lifetime extending to 875,000 km or 15 years for the relevant durability framework.

Official source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1257.

European Union — VECTO and heavy-duty CO₂

The European Commission describes VECTO as the simulation tool for determining heavy-duty vehicle CO₂ emissions and fuel consumption.

Inputs include:

  • engine performance;
  • gearbox friction;
  • auxiliary power;
  • vehicle mass/inertia;
  • rolling resistance;
  • aerodynamic drag;
  • mission profiles.

Commission Regulation (EU) 2017/2400 remains the current VECTO/type-approval framework in 2026, with a consolidated version dated 1 January 2026.

Official sources:

  • European Commission VECTO
  • Regulation (EU) 2017/2400 — 2026 consolidation

European Union — fleet CO₂ targets

Regulation (EU) 2019/1242, as amended, sets fleet CO₂ performance targets for heavy-duty vehicles.

The headline Article 3a reduction levels relative to the 2019 reference include:

  • 45% for 2030–2034;
  • 65% for 2035–2039;
  • 90% from 2040.

The Annex contains vehicle-subgroup implementation details, so program calculations must use the actual applicable subgroup provisions rather than only these headline figures.

Official source: Regulation (EU) 2019/1242, current EUR-Lex consolidation path.

United States — MY2027 heavy-duty criteria pollutants

EPA’s heavy-duty engine and vehicle criteria-pollutant rule establishes more stringent standards beginning with model year 2027 and expands the range of operating conditions and useful-life requirements compared with the earlier program.

Official source: EPA heavy-duty engine and vehicle standards.

United States — July 2026 proposed amendments

On 14 July 2026 EPA published proposed amendments to selected MY2027-and-later heavy-duty highway engine compliance provisions and test procedures, including proposed changes related to useful life, warranty and SCR-inducement provisions.

At the date of this appendix these are proposed, not final.

Official source: EPA July 2026 proposed rule.

United States — 2026 highway-vehicle GHG change

EPA states that on 12 February 2026 it finalized rescission of the 2009 motor-vehicle GHG endangerment finding and repealed the associated federal highway-vehicle GHG standards.

This means a technical publication should not silently carry forward an older statement that those federal highway GHG standards remain unchanged.

Official source: EPA commercial trucks and buses regulation overview.

California

California maintains a separate heavy-duty low-NOx / Omnibus regulatory framework. Federal and California requirements should not be treated as interchangeable.

Official source: California Air Resources Board heavy-duty Omnibus / low-NOx information.
